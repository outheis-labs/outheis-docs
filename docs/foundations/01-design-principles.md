# Design Principles

*On building tools that support life instead of consuming it.*

---

## Attention is all you got

Attention cannot be saved, multiplied, or transferred. Every moment of attention spent is gone.

Most software is designed to capture as much of it as possible. Notifications, feeds, badges — not bugs, but features. The longer you stay, the more is extracted.

outheis does the opposite.

## Core Principles

### Invisible when possible

The best tool is one you don't notice. outheis handles what it can in the background — no dashboards to check, no feeds to scroll, no daily summaries demanding your attention.

### No artificial urgency

No notifications unless you ask for them. No badges, no red dots, no "you have 5 unread items." You decide when to engage.

### Completion over engagement

Success means finishing and leaving, not staying longer. outheis helps you do the thing, then gets out of the way.

### Decision support, not decision replacement

outheis surfaces information, suggests options, remembers context. It doesn't decide for you. You remain the author.

### Local and private by design

Your data stays with you. Not because privacy is a feature — because your life is not a resource to be extracted.

## Why This Matters

Digital tools promise to extend our capabilities. But most of them extract more than they give — attention, data, cognitive sovereignty.

A tool that truly serves you should make space for what matters: presence, focus, the things that require your full attention.

outheis is built on this premise: software should support life, not consume it.
